## 2. The headline result

On a like-for-like basis the equity strategy trails index funds and does not beat
them on risk-adjusted return. Its real feature is a much shallower worst loss.
**CAGR** is the yearly growth rate a return compounds to. **Sharpe** is return
per unit of bumpiness (higher is smoother); here it is measured above a 3.5%
cash rate, so a book that just sat in cash would score 0. **Worst drawdown**
(MaxDD) is the deepest fall from a previous high to the low that followed it,
as a percentage of that high.

| 2012-01-04 → 2026-10-15, AUD | CAGR | Sharpe (vs 3.5% cash) | Worst drawdown |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Strategy portfolio, all 4 sleeves** — survivorship-biased upper bound | 8.35% | 0.59 | −10.0% |
| Benchmark: index-fund ETF blend, dividends reinvested — not survivorship-biased | 12.50% | 0.72 | −29.3% |
| Benchmark: price-index blend, no dividends — not survivorship-biased | 9.29% | 0.49 | −30.6% |

**What this means in plain English.** Buying and holding a blend of ordinary
index funds would have grown money about 4 points a year faster than the
strategy (12.50% vs 8.35%) and would still have scored slightly better on
smoothness. What the strategy buys instead is a much calmer ride: its worst
fall was about a third as deep (−10.0% vs −29.3%), because it only keeps
roughly 40% of the portfolio invested at any time (its "beta", or co-movement
with the market, measures 0.41). That is a trade-off an investor might
reasonably pay for, not a sign the trading signal is adding return.

The cause is exposure, not signal: the book averages 34–44% invested. **All of
these numbers are survivorship-biased upper bounds** because no region has a
point-in-time membership file — the backtest can only pick from today's
company/fund list, which excludes everything that has since been delisted or
dropped from an index, and that tends to flatter the past.

*Re-run at the merge (2026-11-13, through 2026-11-13, commit `3e50f4a`): strategy 8.32% / 0.59 /
−10.0%, ETF blend 12.70% / 0.73 / −29.3%, active return −4.37%, beta 0.41. The extra month of data
moves only the second decimal. That run is what the dashboard's BACKTEST tab now shows.*

*Old figures, for anyone who saw them before this block: the table above used
to read 6.1%/0.32, 8.6%/0.62, 9.6%/0.50, 11.3%/0.65. What moved each one, task

## 13. Small known issues, deliberately left for later

The final review of the block graded every one of these as minor: none makes a reported number wrong
or puts a live book at risk, so none was fixed before the merge question. In rough order of usefulness:

- **The price cache can keep a half-failed download.** If Yahoo refuses some tickers, `load_prices`
  caches what it got, so a backtest or research run can miss names until the cache expires. It is
  loud (the `dead_tickers` alert fires) and the live paper books never use the cache. Related: if a
  region's *index* fails to download, `load_region` crashes with a KeyError instead of saying so.
- **Alerts repeat every run.** `notifications.notify` has no "already said that" memory, so a renamed
  ticker alerts on every scheduled run until `universes.py` is fixed.
- **A dead stock the book still holds cannot be sold** (it has no price), so its dividend window
  keeps reopening and its alert repeats. Selling or writing off a dead holding is a policy decision.
- **A false stock-split halt has no clean exit.** The alert asks for the position and fills to be
  restated; after a dividend drift or a spin-off there is nothing to restate. Rare (it needs about
  three years of holding a high-yield name without trading it).
- **The live broker path's cash check ignores trading costs** (`execution_ibkr`), so on a tight book
  it could buy one more share than the paper book would. Pinned by a test marked expected-to-fail.
- **The comparison backtest's interest is re-computed** by the bridge from the backtest's holdings
  (same rule, proven equal), rather than recorded by the backtest the way its commission now is.
- **CAGR in the backtest's own report is measured over one fewer day** than its Sharpe and
  volatility (about 1/N — negligible over 14 years).
- **The overview page computes total return inline** instead of through `attribution.since_inception`
  (same number today).
- The paper's optional monthly sleeve true-up (`PAPER_ALLOCATION_REBALANCE`, off by default) moves
  cash without a ledger row, which the bridge's cash rebuild would not see if it were switched on.

Found during Task 26 (2026-11-13):

- **A hand-run paper pass during market hours decides on a live, unfinished bar.**
  `engine.run_once` calls `paper_trade.run_daily` with no market-hours check; the scheduler is safe
  only because its cron (21:30 UTC) fires after all four markets close. A manual run at 09:40 UTC
  staged FTSE on 2026-11-13's still-trading bar.
- **`verify --strict` fails on a book no run has evaluated yet.** A freshly opened sleeve reads
  `never-traded` ERROR ("never been through a rebalance"), so the strict audit exits 1 until the
  first daily run. After one pass every sleeve reads `awaiting-fill` INFO (rehearsed on a scratch
  copy: 0 errors, exit 0). Only a manual audit straight after `--init` sees it.
- **"Three sleeves" wording is stale in three places** since TSX was funded:
  `trading_algo/__init__.py:3`, the `paper_trade.init_account` docstring ("the global 3-region
  split") and a comment in `tests/test_alloc_rebalance.py:71`.
